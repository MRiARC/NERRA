THE BRASS ALCHEMIST OF PRAGUE
A Novel of Mystery and Mechanical Intrigue
By Sylvia Locke & Felix Moreau

============================================================

CHAPTER 1: The Ouroboros Cog

The mist that rose from the Vltava River in the damp autumn of October 1891 smelled of wet cobblestone, coal smoke, and old copper. High above the winding lanes of Prague's Old Town, the astronomical clock struck nine, its mechanical apostles gliding behind their gothic portals with the hollow rasp of blackened gears.

In her subterranean atelier beneath Charles Bridge, Sylvia Locke adjusted her brass jeweler’s loupe. She had auburn hair bound tightly with a tarnished copper clasp and keen slate-grey eyes that could discern a stripped gear tooth at three inches. On her workbench lay a mechanical relic unlike any timepiece in the Austrian Empire: a brass scarab the size of a swallow’s egg, its carapace etched with interlocking astrological glyphs. The guild called it the Ouroboros Cog.

"Seventy-two hours, no more," Sylvia whispered to herself, using a bone-handled pipette to feed three droplets of distilled vitriol oil into the scarab’s dorsal escapement. By the immutable canon of Master Locke’s workshop, the clockwork mechanism would freeze irrevocably if deprived of vitriol beyond three days.

The bell above the heavy oak door chimed with a frantic rattle.

Felix Moreau stepped inside, shaking rain from his woolen trench coat. Felix was a scholar of antiquities from the Sorbonne, tall and lean, with ink-stained fingers and an emerald signet ring on his left hand.

"They are searching the alleys by the cathedral, Sylvia," Felix said breathlessly, bolting the iron latch behind him. "Inspector Heinrich Varner has closed the river gates. The Imperial Ministry knows the relic was smuggled out of the Hofburg vault in Vienna."

Sylvia did not look up from her tweezers. "Inspector Varner believes whatever magistrate pays his stipend. But he does not know the secret my father took to his grave."

Felix stepped closer, resting his gloved palms on the scarred oak table. "And what secret is that?"

Sylvia hesitated, her slate-grey eyes clouding. She could not bring herself to confess the truth—that Master Locke had not died of an ordinary fever, but had willingly swallowed silver mercury to seal the cipher within his own petrified veins. "Only that the scarab is not a weapon, Felix. It is a key."

CHAPTER 2: The Hand That Holds the Key

By the third hour after midnight, the rain had turned into a stinging sleet across the Old Town square. Sylvia wrapped the Ouroboros Cog in oiled deer hide and tied it with waxed cord.

"You must take it across the river," Sylvia instructed, placing the heavy bundle into Felix’s hands. "My workshop is the first place Varner will raid when morning breaks. Hide it beneath the floorboards in your lodgings at the Golden Crown Inn."

Felix closed his fingers around the brass artifact. "If I am stopped with Imperial contraband, Sylvia, they will send me to the fortress prison at Spielberg."

"Take the gardener’s tunnel beneath the monastery," she urged. "And whatever occurs, do not wind the counter-pinion without vitriol oil. If the spring snaps, the cipher inside melts."

Felix nodded, pocketing the bundle into the deep breast pocket of his woolen coat. As he reached for the latch, he paused. "Will you be safe here alone?"

"I am an apprentice horologist, Felix. When soldiers demand answers, I play the fool who only knows how to mend carriage clocks."

Felix slipped out into the fog-drenched alleyway, his boots echoing against the wet stone. Sylvia watched from the grated cellar window until his silhouette vanished into the mist. Alone in the quiet cellar, she picked up her father's ledger, turning to the cipher page that neither Felix nor the Imperial Ministry had ever laid eyes upon.

CHAPTER 3: The Raid at the Golden Crown

Dawn arrived without sun, bleeding a dreary pewter grey over the spires of the city. At seven o'clock on the morning of October 15, the sound of splintering pine shattered the silence of the Golden Crown Inn.

Inspector Heinrich Varner kicked open the door to Felix Moreau’s second-floor garret. Varner was a broad-shouldered Bohemian with cold blue eyes, a bristling grey mustache, and a heavy sabre clanking at his hip. Three Austrian gendarme riflemen followed in his wake, bayonets fixed.

"Monsieur Moreau," Varner barked in gutter German, leveling an ebony cane at Felix’s chest. "Step away from the escritoire."

Felix stood frozen in his dressing gown, hands raised. The gendarmes ransacked the room, overturning the mattress, tearing parchment manuscripts from the shelves, and sweeping inkwells to the rug.

Within five minutes, a young corporal gave a triumphant shout from the hearth. Prying up a loose flagstone near the chimney, he lifted out the oiled deer-hide pouch.

Varner unwrapped the bundle, his cold eyes widening as the brass scarab gleamed in the dull morning light. "The Emperor will be pleased," Varner murmured, weighing the Ouroboros Cog in his palm. "Place Monsieur Moreau in iron shackles and transport this mechanism directly to the central precinct lockbox on Celetná Street."

"You don't understand the mechanism, Inspector!" Felix shouted as two soldiers seized his arms. "If it is not oiled with vitriol by tomorrow evening, the internal escapement will self-destruct!"

"The Ministry has its own mechanicians, scholar," Varner sneered. "They do not require advice from a Parisian fugitive."

CHAPTER 4: The Broken Lockbox

Word reached Sylvia by mid-afternoon through a street urchin who delivered news for copper kreuzers. Felix was locked in the city gaol, and the Ouroboros Cog rested in the iron safe of the municipal precinct on Celetná Street.

Desperate, Sylvia climbed the stone stairs to the clinic of Dr. Alistair Mercer on the edge of the Jewish Quarter. Mercer was an expatriate physician from Edinburgh, an aging man with wire-rimmed spectacles and chemical burns across his knuckles. He had been Master Locke’s closest confidant.

"You should not have come here, child," Mercer said in a low voice, locking the clinic door behind her. "Varner's men were questioning the apothecary two hours ago."

"They have Felix," Sylvia said, pacing between the glass specimen jars. "And they took the scarab. If the spring unwinds without vitriol, my father’s life work is lost forever."

Dr. Mercer took off his spectacles and polished them with a silk handkerchief. "Your father was a reckless fool, Sylvia. He believed he could harness the subterranean steam conduits beneath the Vltava without awakening the sleeping guild."

"He gave his life to protect the secret!" Sylvia countered sharply.

Mercer stared at her with a piercing gaze. "You still believe he died of a fever, don't you?"

Sylvia stiffened. Her throat tightened. "He drank the mercury tincture. I saw the empty vial in his hand the night he stopped breathing."

Mercer blinked, genuine astonishment washing over his lined face. "You knew? All this time, you knew he took his own life?"

"I hid the vial before the parish priest arrived," Sylvia whispered, her slate-grey eyes brimming with anger. "Now help me get into the precinct safe. You still have the master skeleton key you forged for the magistrate's hall."

Mercer was silent for a long moment. Then, with a slow nod, he reached into his waistcoat. "I will not let you walk into the gendarmerie, Sylvia. I will go myself. But when this night ends, we leave Bohemia for good."

CHAPTER 5: The Subterranean Foundry

Midnight struck across Prague under a canopy of starless black. The river had swollen, its dark waters rushing against the stone piers of Charles Bridge with a muffled roar.

Sylvia waited beneath the arch of the water gate, clutching a vial of vitriol oil and a brass pocket lantern. Footsteps splashed through the damp conduit behind her. She raised the lantern, expecting Dr. Mercer.

Instead, Felix Moreau emerged from the tunnel shadows, his linen shirt torn and his wrists bruised from iron manacles.

"Felix!" Sylvia gasped, running to him. "How did you escape the precinct cell?"

"Someone blew the iron hinges off the lockbox room with fulminate powder," Felix replied, catching his breath. "The whole station was engulfed in smoke. But Mercer didn't bring the scarab to you, Sylvia. I saw him running down into the Old Foundry sluice."

Sylvia felt a cold tremor race down her spine. "Mercer didn't want to save the relic from Varner. He wanted the subterranean conduits for himself."

Together, they descended the spiral iron stairs into the flooded foundry beneath the city. Steam hissed from antique copper pipes, and green phosphorus moss illuminated the vaulted brick ceiling. At the center of the subterranean chamber, standing upon the stone sluice gate, was Dr. Mercer.

In Mercer’s hands, the Ouroboros Cog was ticking with a rapid, metallic fury. The seventy-two hours had almost expired. The miniature wings of the scarab had unfurled, revealing tiny silver needle-valves that aligned with the brass lock of the great river gate.

"Stay back, Sylvia!" Mercer shouted over the roar of rushing water. "Your father intended this machine to drown the lower district and cleanse the empire of its greed! It was never a key to treasure—it is the floodgate release!"

Sylvia pulled the vitriol vial from her pocket. "If you turn that dial without lubricating the balance arbor, the release valve will jam and the pressure will rupture the boiler under our feet!"

Felix stepped forward, holding out his hands. The clock above the city chimed quarter-past twelve. The gears of the scarab gave a sharp, metallic shriek, and the fate of Prague hung upon a single drop of vitriol.
